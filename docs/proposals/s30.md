# s30 — smooth, glitch-free waterfronts (walkways, terrain, sculpting)

Playtest screenshots 54–57 (waterfront park on a curved lake shore, "Promenade boardwalk" rated D−): the paved promenade's
water edge zig-zagged and stepped, the promenade dipped up and down, the water-side wall was many short quay/railing
pieces at different heights and angles with gaps and overlapping ends, vertical torn terrain "curtains" stood where terraform
patches and nets met at different heights, mounds/cones further back, the timber boardwalk wiggled in S-curves with a second
railing line behind it and a pier crossing it, the bank was a patchwork of sand/grass/rock with seams, and pale sand blotches
lay on the paving. User: *"If you just path near the water in a smooth line, it'll work itself out … I don't like glitches."*
And (56): *"I don't mind that concrete cutout — that happens when you plant a road on a slope. Just make sure we're aware of
it and can control it."*

## What was causing the glitches

1. **The walk played to every wiggle of the shore.** Its line was the real waterline offset (lightly smoothed): every 10–25 m
   wiggle of a natural waterline became a zig-zag of the walk and of the paving edge (`StripFromWater` bands), and an offset
   of a jagged line folds into cusps at every inside bend.
2. **The level was re-fitted locally.** The promenade height followed the ground with ±16 m smoothing and 6 % grade, then
   ramps, landing pads, step levels, kiosk pads and grade relaxation were carved into the bank after it — a promenade dipping
   up and down, and the band under the walk tilted. A CS2 net flattens the ground across its own width, so a tilted band (or
   a path on a cross slope) leaves a vertical lip along the net's edge.
3. **The edge was cut into pieces.** The quay/walkway/boardwalk was split by every link, pier, step flight, cliff probe and
   the "gap" list, then emitted as curved Bézier pieces of a prefab that is built straight (Bridge/Quay `StraightEdges`):
   short wall pieces at different angles, gaps and overlapping ends. The deck level and the ground behind it differed where
   the field had to fade (outline, ramps), so the quay's land side showed its wall and the net cut a sheet into the ground.
4. **Patches at different heights.** Flat `Level` patches (river steps) inside a sloped field, the 4 m sculpt grid (a 3–8 m
   walk cannot be level across on 4 m bilinear cells), the build raster's 4 m bank extrapolating the outermost patch's change
   (a berm in front of a quay wall), a waterfront field that was cut off abruptly where the shore's arc length jumps at the
   outline's corners, and walk curves that hooked at their ends (a sign flip in the distance field) — each a height jump over
   one 3.5 m heightmap texel, which CS2 renders as a stretched vertical sheet.
5. **Texture patchwork.** Lawn surface, sand paint with a noisy edge and dirt on the bank; "wet sand"/"beach" decals on the
   paving because the shore decal rule treated every waterfront as sand.

## The new approach

**`Core/Layers/Types/Beach/ShoreWalk`** — one smooth curve at one smooth height.
- `FromWater`: the real waterline sampled, **smoothed first** (Gaussian by arc length, σ ≈ 9–24 m, 3 passes), then offset along
  its own smooth normal (loop-free), pushed inland where it would come too close (the push dilated/smoothed so it never makes
  a bump), cut to the land, `TrimHooks` drops ends that turn round the outline's corners; `SmoothFromWater` raises σ until the
  radius is ≥ 40 m (`MinCurveRadius`).
- `Profile`: the ground under the curve (median across), smoothed ±40 m, clamped to a band, grade ≤ 2 % both ways.
- `Signed` (distance to the curve extended by rays at its ends, continuous), `Offset`, `Strip`; metrics `MinRadius`,
  `Wiggles` (turn-direction flips on sharp bends), `MaxGrade`.

**Waterfront (`WaterfrontLayer`, rewritten planning; ids/presets stable)**
- ONE walk curve per stretch of shore (the longest piece). Soft edges (natural bank, boardwalk): the walk is set back from the
  water and kept low — never more than a ≤ 10 % bank above the water in front of it — so a straight link can run down the
  bank; where the ground behind rises higher the hillside is cut. Hard edges (quay, walkway): the quay curve first (its sea side
  on the waterline, σ 9 m, radius ≥ 40 m), the walk behind it at a constant distance for a ≤ 8 % bank (harbour: one level).
- ONE terrain field (2 m node grid, sloped quads, emitted once): per node the signed distance from the walk (and from the
  quay), the walk height there and the original ground → flat across the paved band and 1.5 m shoulders; a smoothstep bank to
  the water / down to the quay deck (the deck fill eases to the bed under the deck's outer half, ending at the wall line); a
  smoothstep bank behind as wide as the height it makes up; past the walk's ends and before a cliff stretch it fades out; the
  outline cap ≤ 15 %. No smoothing, grade relaxation or earthwork clamp after the layout was placed on it
  (`ShoreGround.FinishField`); a 2-cell skirt of zero-change patches so the build raster's own bank never extrapolates past
  the field (`Sculpt.Emit(skirt)`).
- **Hillside** (new controls, appended): `hillside` = auto / retaining wall / graded bank, `maxWall` (default 3 m). Wall: the
  walk net runs along the band's land edge at its own height (explicit, like a road planted on a slope) and the ground
  landward of its centre stays original — the game's net cut draws ONE continuous retaining wall along it. Auto picks the wall
  when the cut is between 1.5 m and the limit, else a bank; a forced wall above the limit falls back to a bank with an Info.
  Facts `hillsideWall`, `wallHeightMax`, `wallLength`, `hillsideRise` (tests, gallery, debug dump).
- The edge (quay / walkway / boardwalk) is ONE continuous run along its curve at one deck height, as equal ~8 m straight
  chords sharing their nodes; never interrupted (links and piers join it at nodes). A quay is trimmed to where the ground
  behind it really is at deck level (and the field laid again), never a free-standing end. The boardwalk is parallel to the
  walk at the smoothed water distance + 2.6 m, radius ≥ 40 m, only over water.
- Links: straight paths down the smooth bank only where it is ≤ ~17 % anywhere (boardwalk: down to where the bank reaches deck
  level, then a short level timber stub — a T junction). Piers start ON the quay/boardwalk (T junction) or, on a natural bank,
  at the bank foot reached by a straight link. River steps: paved flights draped on the smooth bank with a path down the
  middle (no level patches). No ramps, landing pads or kiosk pads carved anywhere.
- Railing: ONE continuous chain along the paved band's water edge where the ground drops > 1 m within 3.5 m (gaps ≤ 16 m
  bridged, runs < 12 m dropped, equal 6 m pieces). Cliff-top railings the same (`CliffTop`).
- Ground: paving is a strip along the walk curve (harbour: from the quay), the lawn starts at the band's smooth land edge
  (`Behind`), the bank one lush-grass paint, a clean sand/dirt band only at a soft waterline. Trees, beds and lamps parallel
  to the walk.

**Beach**: the promenade is the same smooth curve (σ 24 m, radius ≥ 40 m, ±40 m / 2 % level), 2 m node grid, access ramps
start at the bench's sea edge with a 3 m level strip and banks widening with the cut, the bank behind the sand starts behind
the bench, dunes only where the ground is not cut deeply, the carve never deeper than the earthwork limit (the beach rides up
parallel to a high bank instead of leaving a shelf), walk benches re-laid just before emission (`ShoreGround.ReapplyBenches`).

**Shared**: `ShoreGround.Connect` levels entrance ramps across `hw + 1.5 m` with banks widening with the cross slope and a
level landing across the promenade band (`landing`); a cliff stretch needs ≥ 16 m of cliff stations (one misread station
locked an island of nodes); `Shoreline.Nearest` is pruned (bounding boxes + warm start) and `Segment` is a binary search
(the 2 m grids ask ~15–20 k times); the shore kit's walks are smoothed by arc length; no sand decals on waterfront paving.

## Metrics and tests

`Core/Build/TerrainMetrics`: **curtain** — texel pairs (3.5 m, the CS2 heightmap) the build made steeper than 50 % by adding
more than 0.6 m of jump, off the nets (a net's own cut or wall covers its width); **seat** — ground difference across a
terrain-following net (edge to edge) and net-vs-ground at the control points, a quay's land side vs its deck. Plan facts
`walkMinRadius`, `walkWiggles`, `walkMaxGrade`, `edgeMinRadius`, `edgeWiggles`, `edgeRuns`, `railingRuns`.

`Tests/Placemaker.Tests/Types/SmoothShoreTests.cs` — every beach and waterfront preset on every shore site (flat, 6.5 m bank,
headland, 13 m cliff, and the new **curved** lake bay with a jagged 10–25 m waterline wiggle): no walk zig-zags, walk radius
≥ 40 m, grade ≤ 2 %; edge radius ≥ 40 m, one edge run per stretch; 0 terrain sheets (natural ground steeper than 30 % — the
headland's cliff transition — not measured); paths level across (≤ 0.45 m) away from the outline; railings in continuous
chains; no flat terraform patches on a waterfront. Plus: the metrics themselves (a jagged line has ≥ 10 wiggles and smoothing
removes them; a 2.5 m terrace step is a curtain, a 30 % slope and a natural cliff are not; a cross slope unseats a path), the
curved bay keeps the walk on land 2 m+ from the water, and the dump format round-trips. `ShoreSculptTests` (s20) updated:
stepped samples off the nets and above the water, curved site, waterfront cut ≤ 6.6 m (the walk stays low and cuts the
hillside behind), beach ≤ 6 m, quay within 2.7 m of the water (it rides over the small wiggles). Older waterfront tests updated
(walk position, no level step patches, piers start on the quay).

Gallery: `--water beach,waterfront --shores flat,bank,headland,cliff,curved`; the summary line now prints the walk/edge
radius, wiggles, grade, edge runs, railing runs, hillside wall/height and `curtain max@x,z/sheets seat cross/gap@x,z` with
the worst net; the top view circles the worst curtain (red) and seat (magenta). Every beach and waterfront render on the five sites: 0 sheets.

## Debug: dump terrain after build (in game)

Options → Placemaker → Maintenance → **"Debug: dump terrain after build"** (off by default). After every finished build it
writes `%USERPROFILE%\AppData\LocalLow\Colossal Order\Cities Skylines II\ModsData\Placemaker\debug\group-<id>-<time>.json`:
the ground before the build (sampled at build start) and after it (at Finish, after the terraform passes and Apply), water
depth from the live surface, the planned terraform target, the group's live nets (curves, width, prefab, explicit/quay) and
objects, the outline and the plan's facts; the log line gives the curtain/seat metrics of the live ground. Render it offline:

```
dotnet run -c Release --project Tests/Placemaker.Gallery -- --terrain-dump "<...>\ModsData\Placemaker\debug" --out <dir>
```

→ `<name>.png` (hillshade, water, contours, nets, objects, every curtain as a red tick, worst seat circled), `<name>-cut.png`
(cut blue / fill red), `<name>-3d.png` (oblique view and cross-sections: before dashed grey, after brown, water blue).
Format: `Core/Build/TerrainDump` (versioned, grids in centimetres).

## Needs in-game verification

- The hillside wall: an explicit-height pathway (FollowTerrain = false) against higher ground draws the concrete retaining
  wall as in screenshot 56 (seen with roads; pathways assumed the same). If it does not, set Hillside to Graded bank.
- Quays as straight 8 m chords sharing nodes: one visually continuous wall with small angle changes; links/piers joining at
  nodes open the railing there (T junctions).
- The waterfront's lush-grass bank paint and the sand band read as one material; no sand decals on the paving.
- The 2 m terraform grids (more, smaller sloped patches: ~5–15 k per 5 ha) build within the direct-terraform time cap.
- Use the dump on the playtest lake: the log's `curtain … sheets` should be 0 and `seat cross` ≲ 0.4 m.
