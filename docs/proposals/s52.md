# s52 — the hillside planner: Roads pattern "Hillside terraces"

User (image 83: two winding roads drawn with the vanilla Continuous tool along a hill's contour lines): *"Place roads on
contour lines for height, so it follows the contour of a mountain — but not just that. Before it even places roads, it
flattens out TERRACES, at least where the road's grid lines are: aware of the road's width and the zoning grid depth
sticking out of the road (8 m cells × up to 6 deep), and it flattens it smooth. The flattening is terraced on the lines —
not blocky. It terraces its way all the way up the hill. It won't terrace a place that's already flat. Settings for all
of that. Avenues going up and down — maybe slithering/snaking switchbacks. And the painting and pedestrian pathing all
incorporated. Basically the ultimate hillside planner."*

A new value of the Roads **Pattern** control, `terraces` ("Hillside terraces"), and four presets. Everything lives in
`Core/Layers/Roads/Terraces/` and runs inside the roads planner, so the network design passes of s34 (connectivity to
the city, dead ends, junction geometry, gravel tips, pre-zoning) apply to it as to every other pattern.

## Design

**One terrace** = a contour street, the zone cells on both sides (`Terrace depth`, 1–6 cells of 8 m) and a 2 m level
shoulder: the band is `road half + cells·8 + 2` each side. The road strip itself is level across (CS2 levels a net across
its width); the rest of the band tilts by the **cross-fall** (default 1 %) away from the bank so rain runs off — the
uphill edge a little higher, the downhill edge a little lower, one plane through the road.

**Spacing from the physics of banks.** Between two terraces the bank has to make up the height step. On ground of slope
g a street every S metres steps ΔH = g·S; the two bands take W = 2·band of it, the bank the rest, so the bank's mean slope
is ΔH / (S − W). Holding it at the mean bank slope a (setting / 1.15 for a rounded bank, the setting for a wall):
S = W·a / (a − g). The level step ΔH = g·S is taken at the site's reference slope (60th percentile of the sloped ground):
streets at a constant height interval, as hill towns are built. Where the hill is steeper the terraces narrow (each
station measures the gap to the next street up and down and keeps what the bank leaves — `TerraceStreet.BandUp/Down`),
where it is gentler the banks are wider and gentler. A hill too steep for the chosen depth gets a Warning ("fewer cells
or a steeper bank fit better").

**Streets** (`TerraceLayout`): the ground is surveyed on a 3–12 m grid and smoothed (σ ≥ 12 m: the lumps go, the hill
stays). The levels' offset is chosen to keep the most street; each level's contour lines (marching squares) are clipped to
the area, ends that run along the outline trimmed, smoothed by arc length until the tightest bend is a street's radius
(≥ 40 m, 6 × the half width), loops too small to be a street dropped, and pieces too close to an accepted street (two road
verges and the bank the step needs) or to a building (its verge and the bank its cut or fill needs) cut out. Streets are
level at their contour height. **A site that is not sloped** (< 20 % of it steeper than the flat threshold, or < 3 m of
relief) gets straight rows along its long axis, back to back at two zone depths, and no terraform at all (Info: "the
ground here is flat").

**Links up the hill** (`TerraceRoutes`), every `Link spacing` along a street (staggered level to level), to the street a
ray up the slope meets. Every link has a **level 12 m landing** at both ends — junctions on terraces are level — and climbs
just under `Steepest road` in between (`Profile`: heights spread by climb weight, landings 0, turns ½):
- **Direct**: straight up the slope where gentle enough; else a diagonal that leaves at 70°, crosses the band, runs across
  the slope *as an offset of the street it follows* (so on a dome it winds round with the contours instead of cutting a
  chord) and arrives at 70° as far along the next street as the climb needs; switchbacks only where neither fits.
- **Switchbacks**: a landing straight up, a quarter turn, legs along the slope joined by **hairpins** (centre-line radius
  ≥ max(16 m, 2.6 × half width), climbing at half the grade in the turn), a quarter turn and a landing up into the next
  street. As many legs as fit between the two streets; stacked legs need a bank between them no steeper than the banks
  elsewhere (`grade·(2·leg + ½π·r) ≤ bank·(2r − 2 verges)`), so tight hairpins on long legs are widened until it fits.
- **Spiral**: long diagonals that keep turning the same way round the hill, each leaving where the last arrived (a
  crossroads on every terrace): one road winding up the whole hill (it turns the other way rather than leave a terrace
  unlinked; then a switchback, then straight).
- `Avenue links`: the links are avenues. A rescue pass tries every station, up and down, for a street the regular stations
  left alone; whatever still stays alone is the s34 connectivity pass's (a straight link, or dropped with a notice).
- Checks: inside the area, clear of buildings by the bank their cut/fill needs, never crossing a street or another route,
  clear of streets and other routes by their verges *and the bank their height difference needs*, arriving at ≥ 50°, no
  hook bends (radius ≥ 0.6 × the street radius).

**Bank walks** (the existing `Walkways` amount and `Walkway style`; 0 = none): a level path across the zone cells to
the bank foot, **one ramp along the bank face at ≤ 7.5 %** (up to 220 m; it follows the street's curve and is stretched so
the real climb stays at the grade; a zig-zag with 6 m turning landings only where its legs part by ≥ 25°, else stacked
legs would steepen the bank between them), a level path across the next terrace's cells to its street. Where the step is
too deep for a ramp in the bank, a straight path climbs across from street to street at ≤ 7.5 % at ≥ 30° to them (a level
stub off each street first, so it never climbs beside a road), like the
stepped lanes of a hill town (vanilla has no stair path). Walks join their streets after the network design (the street
is split there) and are left out if a road the design added since would cross them or pass at another height.

**The ground** (`TerraceField`) — ONE continuous height field on a 2 m node grid (Common/Sculpt; the cell grows past
60 000 nodes), built from the **final** network and emitted once as sloped quads (`PlannedTerraform.Sloped`), so nothing is
blocky and nothing tears:
- every terrace street is one source: within its band the level plateau (cross-fall from the road edge); beyond it the
  band edge eases back into the hillside over the width its height difference needs at the bank slope (smoothstep, 1.5×
  for a rounded bank, 1.1× for a wall), blended between neighbouring terraces with weights 1/x^p (p = 2 rounded, 1 wall):
  between two terraces a smooth bank, beyond the last one an embankment or cutting, far from every street the hillside;
  terrace edges follow the road curves exactly;
- links, city links, lanes and walks lie on their designed profiles, level across their width + 1.5 m, blended into the
  terraces no steeper than the banks; a terrace street's own road strip is a road too;
- **flat ground is never terraced**: the terraces fade out between 60 % and 100 % of `Flat below` (designed roads keep
  their profile there);
- **limits**: every bank node is held at `Steepest bank` (8 directions, so the true gradient is within it); where two
  protected things meet at different heights the lesser gives way over the distance the step needs at the bank slope — a
  terrace before a walk, a walk before a road, any of them before ground that may not change (outline, buildings, existing
  roads) — so a terrace that cannot be full width narrows next to a road instead of ending in a cliff; the change dies out
  towards the outline, buildings and existing roads (≤ 0.9 × bank slope); nothing is cut or filled past `Earthwork limit`
  (a hard box the bank pass keeps too; Info when it bit). CS2 draws > 50 % over one 3.5 m texel as a torn sheet, so the
  bank setting is held at ≤ 46 %.
- Nets follow the terrain (the game lays them on the built ground) and their inner control heights sit on it at ⅓ and ⅔.

**Zoning** (`Pre-zone new roads`, s34): on terraces only the **level rows** of each zone block — per column of cells, as
many rows from the road as lie level (≤ cross-fall + 2.5 %, or the flat threshold), never more than the terrace's cells —
in the plan (`RoadPreZone.Rects`) and after the build on the live blocks (`RoadPreZone.RuleOf` reads the plan's facts
`roads.preZoneDepth` / `roads.preZoneLevel` and its built ground; `BuildService.PreZone`). Zone blocks on banks and along
links stay unzoned.

**Banks** (`TerraceDressing`): `Paint banks` — dry grass in soft patches on the banks the build makes, worn dirt on the
steepest faces (a wall face mostly dirt): two s33 toolkit paints for the whole hillside, the terraces keep the map's lawn.
`Trees on banks` — clumps on the banks, never on a terrace, a road verge or a walk.

**Readout**: stats Terraces, Level terrace (m²), Earth cut / Earth filled (m³), Deepest cut / Highest fill (m), Steepest
road; facts `terraces.*` (levels, streets, links, hairpins, walks, step, spacing, cut, fill, area, steepest bank, …).

road means none for terraces (a perimeter would climb straight up the hill). Schema-driven: no UI or contract change.

## Tests (`Tests/Placemaker.Tests/RoadsTerraceTests.cs`, 14; all presets × hill / valley / steep)

Every preset terraces every slope (level terrace area, cut/fill readout, permanent-terrain warning); terraces level
(adjacent terrace nodes within cross-fall + 1.5 %) and banks within the setting; no terrain sheets on the ground the build
rasters (TerraformRaster + TerrainMetrics.Curtains); zone cells lie level (every zoned quad's cell centres) and the depth
rule takes only the level rows up to the terrace depth, in the plan and on live blocks; designed roads on the built ground:
terrace streets level, links within the grade + 1.5 %, walks ≤ 8 %; links climb within the grade with level landings that
meet their streets' heights; switchbacks keep their hairpin radius and hold their grade when built; link styles (spiral,
avenues); flat ground never terraced (flat site, 3 % plane, 8 % plane above the threshold, the valley floor); earthwork
within the limit with the Info; settings change the plan (cells → levels and spacing, gentler bank → wider spacing, walks /
paint / trees off, wall → dirt and steeper faces); presets; 20 ha within budget. Existing roads tests run the new pattern
and presets too (one network joined to the city, no bare ends, lang keys, sweep over every area shape, 5 ha budget,
thread-safety); `RoadsPlanTests` now expects 10 patterns.

## Gallery

For terraced plans the summary adds the `terraces.*` facts and the glitch line (curtain / seat), and two images join the
top view and the 3D view with sections: `-cut.png` (cut blue / fill red in 0.5 m bands, 2 m contours of the built ground,
nets, protected-ground clashes ringed green, the worst road-height gap magenta) and `-protect.png` (what the field
protected: terraces green, walks orange, roads grey, what gave way red, locked ground blue). Road-site summaries now grade
roads on the built ground (`rl.HeightAfter`).

Looked at: terraced hillside on the 10 % hillside (four contour streets, level zoned terraces stepping up the section,
banks with tree clumps, curving diagonal links, a bank walk), the dome (two rings and a partial street, links winding
round), the valley (terraces on both sides, the floor untouched, hairpins on the steep side), vineyard terraces (nine
narrow terraces with worn faces, S-shaped links), hill town (three levels, avenues spiralling round the hill), serpentine
village, and the flat site (rows, no terraform).

## In-game checks

1. Roads → Pattern: Hillside terraces on a real hillside (or a preset). The preview shows the terraform heat map; the
   summary the terraces, level terrace area, earth cut / filled, deepest cut / highest fill, steepest road.
2. Build: the terraform runs first (direct path; 2 m sloped quads — about 30–40 k for 25 ha: check the progress bar and
   that it converges), then the roads lie on the terraces. Look for torn terrain sheets at the bank edges (none expected)
   and roads with a concrete side wall (a road on a cross slope — none expected on terrace streets).
3. With "Pre-zone new roads": the log line `roads pre-zoning: … zoned whole` — on terraces only the level rows should be
   zoned (count the rows on a 4-cell terrace: 4); buildings grow level on the terraces.
4. Switchbacks: hairpins drivable (vehicles turn without clipping), links climb ≤ the steepest road setting (net info
   panel slope).
5. Options → Placemaker → Maintenance → "Debug: dump terrain after build", then
   `dotnet run -c Release --project Tests/Placemaker.Gallery -- --terrain-dump "<ModsData>\Placemaker\debug" --out <dir>`:
   curtain sheets 0, seat cross ≲ 0.4 m on the terrace streets.
6. Paint: dry grass / dirt on the banks only, the terraces left as the map's lawn.

## Known limits / next

- No stairs in vanilla: where the step between terraces is deep (≥ ~15 m) a ramp in the bank does not fit and the cut
  across the terraces is too long, so steep sites get few walks (pedestrians use the links' sidewalks). A stair path asset
  (mods) would let walks go straight down the banks.
- The connectivity pass may still add a straight link between pieces the terrace links could not join (a short street, a
  street cut by a building); it is graded by the field but can be steeper than the setting (Steep issue).
- A link or street that reaches the outline meets the existing ground there; the last metres follow it.
- The terrace field is emitted as many small sloped quads (the s30 proposal of a heightfield terraform item would make it
  one raster input).
- Terraces are planned on the area's own slope; a terrace band never extends past the outline.
